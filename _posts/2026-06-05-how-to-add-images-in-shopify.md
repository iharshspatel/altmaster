<p>
  Your product might be exactly what a customer is looking for, but if
  the image doesn't do it justice, they'll scroll right past it. On a
  physical store shelf, people pick things up, read the label, and
  feel the texture. Online, your images do all that work.
</p>
<p>
  So getting this right isn't just about aesthetics. It directly
  affects whether someone buys or bounces.
</p>

<h2 id="why-images-matter">
  Why Adding Quality Images to Your Shopify Store Matters
</h2>
<p>
  There's a reason product photography is one of the first things
  serious store owners invest in. According to data shared by Shogun,
  <a
    href="https://www.businessdasher.com/research/b2b-ecommerce-statistics"
    target="_blank"
    rel="noopener noreferrer"
    >75%</a
  >
  of online shoppers say product images influence their purchasing
  decisions. That's not a small margin, that's nearly everyone who
  lands on your page.
</p>
<p>
  Poor images signal low effort. And low effort, fairly or not,
  signals low quality to a buyer who has never interacted with your
  brand before. On the flip side, clean, clear images build trust
  before a single word is read.
</p>

<h3>What makes a good Shopify product image</h3>
<p>
  A good product image doesn't need to be expensive, it needs to be
  clear, well-lit, and honest. Show the product from multiple angles.
  Use a clean background, preferably white or neutral, so nothing
  competes with the item itself. And keep your image sizes consistent
  across your store, mismatched dimensions make even a well-designed
  store look patchy.
</p>
<p>
  Shopify recommends a resolution of 2048 x 2048 pixels for product
  images to ensure they look sharp on zoom. Supported formats are JPG,
  PNG, GIF, and WebP.
</p>

<h2 id="alt-text">Improve Your Image Rankings with AltMaster</h2>
<h3>What is ALT text, and why does it matter</h3>
<p>
  ALT text, short for alternative text, is a brief description you
  attach to each image on your store. Search engines can't "see"
  images the way humans do, so they rely on this text to understand
  what an image contains and index it accordingly.
</p>
<p>
  It also matters for accessibility. Screen readers used by visually
  impaired shoppers read out ALT text to describe images, making it a
  basic but important part of building an inclusive store.
</p>
<p>
  According to Shopify's own research, 1 in 3 homepage images
  currently has missing, vague, or repetitive ALT text. That's a
  significant gap, and a real opportunity if your competitors are
  ignoring it.
</p>

<h3>How to add ALT text to Shopify images</h3>
<p>
  Adding ALT text manually in Shopify takes just a few clicks. In the
  Media section of any product, click on an image, and you'll see a
  field to enter the ALT text. Keep it descriptive and specific,
  "blue ceramic coffee mug with handle" beats "mug" every time.
</p>
<p>
  A good rule of thumb: write it as if you're describing the image to
  someone over the phone.
</p>
