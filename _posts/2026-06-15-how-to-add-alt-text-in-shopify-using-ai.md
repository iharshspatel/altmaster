<h2 id="why-it-matters">
  Why Alt Text Is a Big Deal for Your Shopify Store
</h2>
<p>
  Most Shopify store owners obsess over their product copy, their
  theme, and their ad spend. Alt text? It's an afterthought, and
  that's exactly why it's an opportunity.
</p>

<h3>It affects your SEO more than you think</h3>
<p>
  Search engines can't see images. They read alt text. Every product
  photo without it is a missed keyword placement, a missed shot at
  appearing in Google Image Search, and a missed chance to tell Google
  exactly what you're selling. For an e-commerce store where images
  <em>are</em> the product, that adds up fast.
</p>

<h3>Accessibility isn't optional anymore</h3>
<p>
  Over 61 million Americans live with a disability. Screen readers
  rely on alt text to describe images to visually impaired shoppers,
  and if your store is silent on that front, you're excluding real
  customers. Beyond the ethics of it, accessibility is increasingly
  tied to legal compliance. Skipping alt text isn't just bad SEO; it's
  a liability.
</p>

<h2 id="can-ai-write">Can AI Really Write Alt Text for You?</h2>
<p>
  Short answer: yes, with caveats. AI is genuinely good at this, but
  it's not flawless.
</p>

<h3>What AI gets right</h3>
<p>
  AI tools can analyze product images and generate descriptive,
  keyword-aware alt text in seconds. For a store with hundreds of
  SKUs, that's the difference between months of manual work and an
  afternoon. Done well, AI-generated alt text hits the right length
  (under 125 characters), includes relevant product keywords, and
  beats the blank field every single time.
</p>

<h3>Where AI falls short (and what to do about it)</h3>
<p>
  The real limitation is context. AI describes what it <em>sees</em>,
  not what it <em>means</em>. It might write "blue garment on a
  hanger" when you need "men's slim-fit navy linen shirt, spring
  collection." It doesn't know your brand voice, your product naming
  conventions, or which keyword you're actually trying to rank for.
</p>

<p>
  The fix? Use AI as the engine, but feed it the right inputs, product
  titles, categories, target keywords, so the output is actually
  useful, not just technically present.
</p>

<h2 id="manual-scale">
  The Problem With Doing It Manually at Scale
</h2>
<h3>100+ products? Here's what that actually looks like</h3>
<p>
  Let's do the math. One product, three images, 30 seconds per alt
  text. That's 90 seconds per product. For a 500-product catalog,
  you're looking at 12+ hours, and that's <em>before</em> new
  arrivals, restocks, or seasonal updates. Most store owners either
  skip it entirely or do it once and forget it. Neither option is
  doing your SEO any favors.
</p>
