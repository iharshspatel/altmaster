<p>
  You've got great products, solid photos, and a store that looks the
  part. But if your images don't have ALT text, Google is skipping
  right over them, and so is every AI-powered search tool that's
  increasingly deciding what shoppers see first.
</p>
<p>

  can do today.
</p>

<h2 id="what-is-alt-text">What is ALT Text?</h2>
<p>
  <strong
    >Think of it as a label Google reads, not your customer.</strong
  >
  ALT text is a short description you attach to an image. Your
  customers never see it; it lives in the background code of your
  store. But Google reads it every time it crawls your site.
</p>
<p>
  Without it, Google sees a filename like
  <code>IMG_4872_final_v3.jpg</code> and has no idea what's in the
  photo. With it, Google knows you're selling a "matte black stainless
  steel coffee tumbler, 16oz, with lid." That context matters: for
  rankings, for image search, and increasingly for AI-driven results.
</p>

<h3>Good vs bad ALT text: a quick example</h3>
<p>Here's the difference in practice:</p>
<div class="alt-example">
  <div class="box bad">
    <span class="box-label">✗ Bad</span>
    <ul>
      <li><span class="sample">product image</span></li>
      <li><span class="sample">blue shirt</span></li>
      <li><span class="sample">(left blank)</span></li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="box good">
    <span class="box-label">✓ Good</span>
    <p style="margin: 0">
      <span class="sample"
        >Men's slim-fit Oxford shirt in navy blue, button-down collar,
        front view</span
      >
    </p>
  </div>
</div>
<p>
  The good version is specific, descriptive, and written like you're
  explaining the image to someone who can't see it. That's genuinely
  the right mental model, because that's exactly what you're doing.
</p>

<h2 id="why-it-matters">Why your Shopify store needs ALT Text</h2>
<p>
  ALT text has been an
  <a
    href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-images"
    target="_blank"
  >
    SEO standard </a
  >for over a decade, which means any store that's done even basic SEO
  work has it covered. If yours doesn't, you're not just missing an
  opportunity; you're actively behind.
</p>
<p>
  Google uses ALT text to understand your product pages, rank your
  images in search results, and build a clearer picture of what you
  sell. A product page with descriptive ALT text on every image sends
  stronger signals than a page with blank or generic ones. When you're
  competing for the same keywords, those signals matter.
</p>

<h3>It's not just SEO: real people need it too</h3>
<p>
  Screen readers used by visually impaired shoppers read ALT text
  aloud. If your images are blank, those customers hear nothing, or
  worse, a garbled filename. In several markets, accessibility is also
  a legal requirement for online stores, not just a best practice.
</p>
<p>
  Writing good ALT text means your store works for everyone. That's
  worth doing regardless of the SEO benefits.
</p>

<h2 id="how-to-add">
  How to Add ALT Text to Your Store Images (Step by Step)
</h2>
<p>
  You don't need an app or a developer. Here's the process inside
  Shopify:
</p>
<ol>
  <li>Go to <strong>Products</strong> in your Shopify admin.</li>
  <li>Open any product and click on an image.</li>
  <li>
    In the dialog that opens, find the <strong>ALT text</strong>
    field.
  </li>
  <li>Write a clear, specific description of what's in the image.</li>
  <li>Hit <strong>Save</strong>.</li>
</ol>
<p>That's it. Repeat across each image on the page.</p>
<p>
  But the real question is how to write ALT text that ranks.
  <strong><em>Imagine describing the image to someone over the phone
  in one sentence.</em></strong> That
  sentence is your ALT text. Mention the product name, the key detail
  shown, the color or material if relevant, and the angle or context.
  Don't keyword-stuff it. One clean sentence is all you need.
</p>
<p>Aim to cover:</p>
<ul>
  <li>
    <strong>Hero image</strong> → product name, material, color, front
    view
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>Detail shots</strong> → the specific feature being shown
  </li>
  <li><strong>Lifestyle images</strong> → who's using it and how</li>
  <li>
    <strong>Size reference shots</strong> → what the scale object is
  </li>
</ul>
<p>
  For a typical product with 4 to 6 images, this takes about 5 minutes
  once you're in rhythm.
</p>
